9. Prioritised recommendations

P1 · Make the website a citable source for buyer questions draft
Finding
Client website cited as a source
Evidence
22% of 9 valid responses (sample-platform-a, sample-platform-b) (Limitation: Small samples; AI answers vary between runs, locations and accounts.)
Why it matters
Even when an AI answer mentions a brand, it may cite a directory or competitor instead of the brand’s own site.
Suggested action
Publish pages that directly answer the tested buyer questions (service + location + specifics). Review which domains are cited today and close the content gaps they reveal.
Effort
Medium–High
Expected outcome
Hypothesis: If the website directly answers the tested questions, the client domain may be cited more often. No outcome is guaranteed.
P1 · Add a genuine FAQ section to key service pages draft
Finding
Question-and-answer content buyers can quote
Evidence
No page with 3+ question headings or FAQPage markup
Why it matters
Buyers ask questions in natural language — the same way they ask AI assistants. Direct answers on your own pages give those systems something accurate to quote.
Suggested action
Collect the real questions your sales team hears and answer them on the relevant service pages. Mark up with FAQPage schema only if the Q&A is visible on the page.
Effort
Medium
Expected outcome
Hypothesis: If the site answers common buyer questions directly, it may be more likely to be cited for them. FAQ markup alone does not guarantee visibility.
P1 · Show business registration details and a privacy policy draft
Finding
Business identity signals (GSTIN, privacy policy)
Evidence
GSTIN shown: no; privacy policy: no
Why it matters
GSTIN and a privacy policy are basic credibility signals for Indian B2B buyers and are expected under India’s data protection rules when collecting enquiries.
Suggested action
Display the GSTIN in the footer (if the business is comfortable doing so) and publish a privacy policy describing how enquiry data is used.
Effort
Low
Expected outcome
Hypothesis: If identity signals are visible, cautious buyers may be more willing to submit an enquiry.
P1 · Name each core service in page titles and headings draft
Finding
Configured services appear in titles/H1s
Evidence
full truckload: body text only; part load: body text only; container transport: not found; ODC transport: not found
Why it matters
Buyers search by service (“part load transport”, “customs clearance”). If the service is only mentioned in passing, the site is unlikely to match those searches or AI answers.
Suggested action
For every service marked ‘not found’ or ‘body text only’, add it to the title and H1 of the most relevant page.
Effort
Medium (half a day)
Expected outcome
Hypothesis: If services are stated explicitly, the site may become eligible to appear for more service-specific queries.
P1 · Create a dedicated page for each core service draft
Finding
Dedicated pages for each core service
Evidence
0 of 4 services have a dedicated page; missing: full truckload, part load, container transport, ODC transport
Why it matters
A single generic ‘Services’ page cannot answer specific buyer questions about each service.
Suggested action
Create one substantive page per missing service: what it covers, vehicle/equipment types, coverage, typical lead times, documents needed, and a quote CTA.
Effort
Medium–High (1 page per day including review)
Expected outcome
Hypothesis: If each service has a focused page, those pages may be cited or ranked for service-specific questions.
P1 · Cover the cities, states and routes you actually serve draft
Finding
Target cities, states and routes covered
Evidence
Mumbai: body text only; Pune: body text only; Nashik: not found; Gujarat: not found
Why it matters
Logistics buyers almost always search with a location or lane. Missing geography means missing the question entirely.

Effort
Medium (depends on number of lanes)
Expected outcome
Hypothesis: If target locations and lanes are covered with real detail, the site may appear for more location-specific queries.

Effort
Medium–High

P2 · Add Organization structured data that matches the page draft
Finding
Organization / LocalBusiness structured data
Evidence
No Organization or LocalBusiness JSON-LD detected
Why it matters
Structured data gives search engines an unambiguous record of the business name, URL, logo and contact points.
Suggested action
Add Organization (or LocalBusiness) JSON-LD with genuine name, URL, logo, phone and address. Never add ratings or reviews that are not shown on the page. Fix any JSON-LD that fails to parse.
Effort
Low (1–2 hours)
Expected outcome
Hypothesis: If the entity is described consistently, search engines may associate the brand with its website more reliably. Structured data does not guarantee rich results or AI citations.

P2 · Publish an XML sitemap draft
Finding
XML sitemap available
Evidence
No sitemap found (checked https://www.sample-roadlines.example/sitemap.xml, status 404)
Why it matters
A sitemap helps search engines find every service, route and location page, not only those linked from the homepage.
Suggested action
Generate /sitemap.xml listing all canonical public pages, reference it in robots.txt and submit it in Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools.
Effort
Low (1–2 hours; most CMSs have a plugin)
Expected outcome
Hypothesis: If a sitemap is submitted, deeper pages such as route pages may be indexed more completely.

Finding
Unique meta descriptions
Evidence
4 of 5 pages have a missing, poorly sized (outside 50–170 chars) or duplicated meta description
Why it matters
Meta descriptions shape the search snippet and help a buyer decide whether to enquire.
Suggested action
Write a 50–170 character description for each page summarising the service, coverage and the next step (call / quote).
Effort
Low (10 minutes per page)
Expected outcome
Hypothesis: If snippets are specific, more qualified searchers may click through.
P2 · Make every page’s canonical URL point to itself draft
Finding
Self-referencing canonical URLs
Evidence
4 of 5 pages lack a canonical tag, point it to another domain, or point it to a different URL; 4 inner page(s) declare the HOMEPAGE as canonical, which asks search engines to ignore them (Limitation: A canonical pointing elsewhere can be intentional for duplicate pages; confirm before changing.)
Why it matters
A missing or wrong canonical can split ranking signals across URL variants — or, if inner pages point to the homepage, tell search engines to ignore those pages entirely.
Suggested action
On each page, set <link rel="canonical"> to that page’s own preferred https URL. Only point elsewhere for genuine duplicates.
Effort
Low (template change)
Expected outcome
Hypothesis: If duplicates consolidate, the preferred URL may accumulate signals more consistently.

Effort

10. Measurement limitations

  • The diagnostic score is RADYNTA’s internal framework for prioritising work. It is not an official score or ranking from Google, OpenAI, Perplexity, Microsoft or any other platform.
  • AI answers vary between runs, users, locations and over time. Visibility results are a sample collected on the dates shown, not a permanent measurement.
  • API-collected answers may differ from what users see in consumer apps. The collection method for each platform is listed in the scope section.
  • Rates use valid responses as the denominator. Failed collections are reported separately and are never counted as ‘not mentioned’.
  • The website crawl covered public pages only, within a fixed page limit. Pages behind logins, forms or JavaScript-only rendering may not have been assessed.
  • Expected outcomes are hypotheses. No ranking, citation or enquiry increase is guaranteed.
  • A1 Brand mentioned in AI answers: Small samples; AI answers vary between runs, locations and accounts.
  • A2 Client website cited as a source: Small samples; AI answers vary between runs, locations and accounts.
  • T8 Self-referencing canonical URLs: A canonical pointing elsewhere can be intentional for duplicate pages; confirm before changing.
  • T9 No broken internal pages among crawled URLs: Only crawled URLs were checked; internal URLs beyond the page limit were not tested.
  • R1 Phone number visible: Numbers rendered only as images or by JavaScript are not detected.
  • R3 Physical address with PIN code: Heuristic detection; a human reviewer should confirm the address is complete and matches the Google Business Profile.
  • C1 Pages with substantive content (250+ words): Word count is a proxy; it does not measure accuracy or usefulness.
  • V3 Enquiry / quote form: Forms injected by third-party scripts after page load may not be detected.
